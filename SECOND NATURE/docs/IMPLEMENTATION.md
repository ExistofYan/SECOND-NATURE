# Participant registration and coursework evidence

Research mode now opens a separate registration page before consent. The researcher types an English/pinyin name and ANU ID; the participant confirms current COMP3900/6390 enrolment and consents with a fresh gesture. Invalid or missing details block continuation. Identity is persisted only after consent; free play and legacy records remain supported. Camera frames and landmarks are not saved.

PDF session details include name, ID, course, consent version/time, original start and story-end times, completion time for all 16 ratings, export time, and the recorded device time zone. Every CSV carries identity and session dates. Embedded export-metadata.json records export time; participant-list.txt provides the course-required participant-list format. Preserve raw records in the access-controlled coursework materials directory, not the public game repository. The multi-session analysis groups people by student ID when available and falls back to legacy codes; a replay does not consume a primary-participant slot.

The complete requirements checklist, nine-part evaluation plan, questionnaire, interview prompts and blank evidence templates are in [the evaluation guide](../materials/SECOND_NATURE_Evaluation_and_Interview_Guide.md). Actual participants, recordings, answers and findings still need to be collected.

# Quantitative evaluation update (v1.3.0)

The post-game questionnaire now has 16 custom single-item ratings, grouped into eight waste-prevention questions and eight interface-experience questions. Each screen presents one question, five fully labelled options and both overall and section progress. All selections remain gesture-only, with a fresh pinch required per response, no typing and no countdown. Responses save immediately and resume at the next unanswered question. Both free play and research sessions use this flow.

Waste items cover single-use understanding, food-waste understanding, prevention versus recycling, perceived consequences of choices, confidence, student-life relevance, reuse intention and food-portion planning intention. Interface items cover ease, perceived responsiveness, self-reported selection accuracy, confirmation feedback, fatigue, story coherence, outcome clarity and engagement. The four previous question keys, wording and scales are unchanged; older sessions retain their scores and offer only missing questions. Each new timestamped rating event also preserves the instrument version, question, all five labels, section, score meaning and current app version. Later responses retain the original story completion time and are identifiable by their event timestamps.

The single downloadable PDF contains separate waste and interface tables with every question, raw score, selected label and all five scale labels. It embeds ratings.csv, summary.csv, rating-questionnaire.json, the unchanged session record, timestamped events and the analysis script. Missing responses stay missing. Higher fatigue means more tiredness. The other questions have their own named direction; heterogeneous items are never added into a total or labelled SUS.

Run `python materials/analyze_story.py materials/P01/session.json materials/P02/session.json` after extraction to produce per-participant rating columns, with per-question valid n, missing n, median, range and 1-5 distributions on stderr. Keep the exported rating-questionnaire.json beside the exported script; the repository also includes the same checked definition. Aggregates use only the completed first primary research record per participant, grouped by story model. Free play and replay rows remain visible but do not inflate participant denominators. A missing answer is not zero. Treat delayed feedback separately when interpreting results, using the original timestamps. Custom self-reports do not establish knowledge gain, observed error rates, measured latency or actual waste reduction. No participant data or study findings are generated by these changes.

# Implementation notes - v1.2.3

## Feedback entry and legacy sessions in 1.2.3

Opening a completed saved day via View / export now opens the next unanswered experience question, rather than silently bypassing feedback. The cover's Continue action also recognizes completed legacy days with missing ratings. The final caption routes to ratings based on the actual missing answers even when an older snapshot stores summary as its next page. Unread final captions and optional research post-checks remain ahead of feedback.

The results pages expose a visible feedback banner and gesture button while answers are missing, and show a saved confirmation after all four. Existing recorded choices, completion time, model/app version and scores are preserved. A timestamped ratings_opened event identifies where the questionnaire was reopened, the current software version and the original story end time, so later feedback can be distinguished from immediate post-game responses. The footer shows v1.2.3 to help identify an outdated running build. Start.cmd opens a versioned URL, and the production build must still be redeployed separately for a hosted website.

## Post-game feedback in 1.2.2

After the final reading panel, both free play and research sessions enter four gesture-only experience questions: selection ease, arm fatigue, story connection and outcome clarity. Optional research post-checks still precede the ratings. Every score from 1 to 5 has a visible meaning; selection requires a fresh pinch, with no typing or timeout. Responses are saved immediately with a timestamped event containing the question wording, scale endpoints and selected label. Rating values are validated and a submitted answer cannot be overwritten by a repeated input.

The next unanswered question is derived from the saved session. Completed-story sessions with unfinished ratings remain resumable from the cover and saved-days list. After all four answers, the normal results and PDF export follow. The PDF shows each rating as n / 5, its scale, and how many of the four questions were answered; the raw session and timestamped events remain embedded. Fatigue is reported in its original direction (higher means more tired), and no combined or SUS score is created. Existing records remain unchanged; uncollected answers are shown as missing.


## Model

The model snapshot travels with each session. Seventeen linked scenes converge to the same sequence; availability is derived from actual carried items. Cup and lunch-kit cleanliness are tracked independently. Each scene has two or three available choices; checkout has four definitions of which three are visible.

Item IDs are stable within a session. Initial belongings are marked existing. The notebook and shirt are initial items, not new purchases. Printing creates sheets in use; final handling decides whether they are retained or discarded. A new bag retained tonight remains a new acquired single-use item. Recycling stays in the discard total. The fruit intended for today is consumed on the way home, recorded at the dinner scene; extra fruit and unused home ingredients remain edible inventory. Cooking marks ingredients as prepared into the meal, rather than pretending all prepared food was eaten. No food and non-food quantities are added into a score.

`commitChoice` validates the active visit and availability, clones the state, records item events linked by `choiceCommitId`, records the choice and advances the checkpoint as one serializable snapshot. Repeated commits return the already-committed state. UI state and reports share the event replay analysis. Derived item events must not be added to choice effects a second time.

## Timing and recovery

The decision clock starts only after the art is prepared, the fade completes and an open hand rearms input. Effective time excludes the union of all pause intervals. Tracking absence and intentional rest are not inferred to be user errors. Known segments are closed on explicit exit, page hiding and selection. A new monotonic segment is created on restore. An abrupt refresh that loses an unclosed fragment marks the scene timing missing; surviving fragment events remain available for audit.

The localStorage snapshot is updated for each choice and session state change. A failed save leaves the memory session available with an explicit export warning. Browser storage is not permanent archival storage: export actual research sessions promptly. Storage capacity depends on the browser/device. Unsupported old storage is not silently migrated.

No camera frames or keypoints enter session events. There is one camera stream and one hand-model detection loop. The existing interpreter uses palm-normalised pinch thresholds, hysteresis and smoothing. One press edge is consumed by only the topmost gesture button. Opening a new page/modal or finishing a transition requires release before a new pinch can act. Mouse click, Enter and Space do not activate game controls.

## Artwork and typography

The current release uses 21 distinct PNG illustrations in `public/art/narrative`: a cover, sixteen everyday locations, a three-way departure overview and separate bicycle, bus and car journeys. The same striped-shirt student and backpack recur across breakfast, campus, shopping and evening scenes. These are flat hand-drawn scenes with muted slate, cream and mustard colours, following the supplied visual reference. The older monochrome SVGs remain available as legacy assets; they are no longer the displayed scene backgrounds.

`public/art/narrative-manifest.json` lists the current illustrations. `scripts/extract-narrative-art.mjs` crops the five original generated storyboard sheets without recolouring them. Normal development and build commands use the checked-in PNGs and do not require those cached source sheets. `ComicScene.vue` selects the departure overview before a travel choice and the chosen journey afterwards; the black reading panel also shows that journey. The departure loader prepares all four travel images before the decision is ready.

`story-theme.css` supplies matching muted surfaces and a desktop layout with the illustration beside large story text and label-only choice cards. Headings use system handwriting fonts, so their appearance still depends on the device. Inventory badges are derived from the ledger; the background illustrations are illustrative and do not dynamically redraw every carried object or portion. The ledger and reports remain authoritative for quantities.

The art preloader caches the current and next two scenes. A five-second asset wait falls back to an explicit textual description and records `degraded_asset` and the visit's asset status. It does not claim a successful full-art trial. Short fades respect the initial reduced-motion preference; the cover gesture toggle changes the next session condition, which is saved with that session.

## Two-hand input in 1.2.0

One background detector now returns up to two hands. `MultiHandInterpreter` keeps a separate pinch history for each hand, matches identities independently of detector array order, and publishes one controlling pointer. A fresh pinch on the other visible hand can take control while the first hand is open. Two simultaneous pinches generate one press, and a held pinch never repeats.

A brief controlling-hand dropout keeps ownership during the 300 ms grace period. After a longer loss, takeover requires a stable open hand before a new pinch. Invalid or undersized palms are treated as unavailable, allowing a usable hand to take over. Losing the only pinched hand forwards one cancellation event. The pointer holds its last open-hand aim during pinch confirmation so fingertip motion does not move a click away from its intended target.

## Research and exports

Play and research sessions are distinct. Consent is required before creating a research session. Declining returns without creating research data. Knowledge checks default off and retain actual answers when enabled. A/B order is researcher-selectable; this does not establish equivalent difficulty. Results follow post-checks and ratings in the normal study flow. Ratings are custom single items, not SUS. Replay creates a free-play child session; primary research uses the first session per participant.

The download is one PDF, generated offline without a print dialog or external dependency. It contains session metadata, item counts by type and destination, rule-based personal interpretations, available alternatives for each submitted choice, actual ratings and knowledge-check responses, decision timing context, and the item ledger. Missing answers remain missing, and partial days are identified. Food servings and non-food items remain separate. No environmental score or personality label is generated.

Original session.json, choices.csv, items.csv, events.csv, summary.csv, choice-review.csv and analyze_story.py are embedded as lossless UTF-8 attachments inside that PDF. Parent-session and comparison data are included when available. Use a PDF reader's attachments panel or `python materials/extract_report.py report.pdf --output materials/P01` to recover the records for the coursework materials folder. Extraction uses only Python's standard library and refuses to overwrite differing existing data. `storyFiles` remains an internal data/replay API; it is no longer downloaded as a ZIP. Nothing generates participant quotations, themes, significance tests or causal conclusions.

## Gesture responsiveness in 1.2.1

The pointer holds its aim during initial pinch and release confirmation, then follows motion again during a sustained pinch. A distance-adaptive filter uses the original 35 ms smoothing for tiny movements and reduces it to 10.5 ms for deliberate movement. The existing 70 ms pinch confirmation and release-to-rearm protections remain. Camera capture requests up to 60 FPS when supported; the detector cap is 60 FPS, with only one frame in flight. On an inference reply, a fresh camera frame can be captured immediately rather than waiting for another camera callback. Slower cameras and inference still determine the achievable rate. These changes do not establish a measured hardware FPS or real-hand usability result.

## Still requires real-world verification

The implemented rules and automated checks do not replace real webcam trials or a visual fit check on the actual classroom screen. Verify reaching every option, open-hand edge scrolling on short screens, arm fatigue, tracking pauses, modal recovery, browser camera permissions, browser-managed downloads and the legibility of the selected system fonts. Check the final course brief separately for participant eligibility and submission requirements.

## Gesture-confirmed transition reading (1.1.1)

After each committed choice, the scene fades to a black reading panel. It shows the consequence and the next location/arrival text. This panel has no auto-advance timer. A fresh pinch on Continue loads and fades in the next scene, or opens the end-of-day flow after the final choice. Reading is excluded from decision and animation timing. Short fades only shorten the animation; they never skip the reading step.

The existing gesture release guard is rearmed when the panel appears and again after continuing, so a held pinch cannot skip the panel or choose the next answer. Loss of tracking while reading allows an arm rest without opening a tracking modal. Explicit pauses and page hiding return to the same reading panel. The saved optional pendingTransition keeps unread feedback across refresh, including the final completed choice.

Story choices display one large label each. Descriptions remain in model snapshots for reports and alternative-choice explanations, but are not rendered as small subtitles on the choice cards. New model comic-day-1.1.1 adds short explicit questions where needed.
